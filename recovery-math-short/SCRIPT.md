## VO SCRIPT (188 words)

**[HOOK]**
> Lose fifty percent. You need a hundred percent to get back. Watch.

**[THE DEMONSTRATION]**
> A hundred dollars. It falls fifty percent — you're at fifty. Now gain fifty percent back.
> Seventy-five. You're still down a quarter.

**[THE REVEAL]**
> To return to a hundred, that fifty has to double. A hundred percent gain, to undo a fifty
> percent loss.

**[THE RULE]**
> The formula is one, divided by one minus your loss. It isn't symmetrical, and it gets worse
> fast. Down ten needs eleven. Down thirty needs forty-three. Down eighty needs four hundred.

**[THE REAL WORLD]**
> October 2007. The S&P 500 fell fifty-seven percent by March 2009. Breakeven needed roughly a
> hundred and thirty percent, and it took until 2013 — five and a half years from the peak.
> The Nasdaq fell seventy-eight percent in the dot-com crash. It didn't see that peak again
> until 2015.

**[THE HONEST COUNTER — mandatory beat]**
> But here's what the math does not say: sell to avoid it. Sidestepping the fall usually means
> missing the climb — and the climb is where the hundred percent lives. This is an argument
> about position sizing, not about panic.

**[CLOSE + CTA]**
> Run the number on your worst position before you average down. This is The Meticulous
> Investor.

### The two numbers most likely to be got wrong — and how this script avoids them
- **Percent-to-recover is not the mirror of percent-lost.** −57% needs +131%, not +57%. The
  whole video exists to make that legible.
- **Peak-to-recovery vs. trough-to-recovery.** Commentary mixes these constantly. The script
  says "five and a half years **from the peak**". From the trough it is about four. Either is
  true; stating which one is the difference between accurate and sloppy.

### Egress note
Market-data and financial-news domains are blocked by this environment's proxy, so items 8–12
were read from search-index snippets rather than primary sources. These are long-settled
historical figures that appear consistently across many independent outlets, which is why
confidence is High despite the indirect route. Items 1–7 need no source — they are arithmetic
and the video shows the working.

graphic, and the central object is physical so the maths is *visible* rather than annotated.

**HERO SHOT:** a stack of 100 identical machined steel blocks. Half shear away and tumble.
Then, to rebuild, 50 more must be set on a pile of 50 — and the stack visibly **doubles** to
reach a ghost outline of where it started. The doubling is the whole idea, in one object.

**BOOKEND:** the full stack. Opens on it complete under cold light; closes on it restored,
with the ghost outline of its collapsed height still marked in red.

**Match-cut chain:** stack → block macro → etched plate → paired bars → carved wall → stack.
**Impact-frame words:** `−50%`, `×2`, `+100%`, `MISSING THE CLIMB`.
**Beat rule:** cut or punch in every 1.2–1.8s. No shot past 2s.

### Why this is "exceptionally easy to produce" and should still look expensive
One object carries the entire video. The stack is built once and then only ever loses blocks,
gains blocks, or is measured against its own ghost outline. There is no location to establish,
no second metaphor to teach, and the arithmetic is legible without a single word of on-screen
explanation. That is what buys the budget for shots 11–15 to be dense.

### Name-tags, not logos
`S&P 500` and `NASDAQ` are plain text name-tags on dark plates. No index logos, no exchange
marks — same rule as every previous short.
