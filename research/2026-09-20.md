## Verification method — read before trusting a link

This container's egress proxy **blocks `WebFetch` and direct `curl` to news and
primary-source domains** — `techcrunch.com`, `en.wikipedia.org`, `spacex.com`,
`blogs.microsoft.com`, `nvidianews.nvidia.com`, `reuters.com`, `arxiv.org` and others all
return `403 CONNECT` or `EGRESS_BLOCKED`. Only `WebSearch` works.

Therefore:
- Every claim below was **corroborated across independent searches**, and the confidence
  column reflects how many outlets agreed.
- **Source URLs are cited from search results, not from pages that were opened.** No claim
  in this file has been read at its primary source in this session.
- Nothing here is marked "verified against primary source", because nothing could be.
- **Owner action:** before publishing, open the links under the four selected stories and
  confirm the figures. They are listed in `scripts/2026-09-20.md` → *Pre-publish
  verification*.

Claims that survived to the script were chosen partly for being **hard to get wrong**:
prices, dates, page counts and field-of-view figures reported identically by several
outlets.

### Why the doubled visual weight changed the lineup
**SoftBank (#13)** is arguably the week's biggest number — a $11.87B loan toward a ~$65B
OpenAI position. It scores 7 on "holy shit" and **2 on visuals**: there is no footage of a
syndicated loan. Twelve seconds of stock-photo skyline and a counter would be the weakest
segment in the episode. Dropped on visuals alone. The same logic sank the quantum
error-correction result (#12), which is a genuinely important milestone with nothing to show.

### Why Starship was held despite a 41
**It has not flown.** Flight 14 was on the FAA advisory for 2026-09-18, then moved; SpaceX
is now targeting **NET 2026-09-22, 12:15 UTC**, backup 09-23. Covering it this week means
covering an *unflown* launch, using file footage of previous flights, with the risk it slips
again between upload and view. It is the strongest story in the pipeline for **next week**,
when it will have flown and there will be first-orbital-flight and Starlink V3 deployment
footage. Held deliberately, not missed.

### The spine of the chosen three
Not a grab-bag. Snap, Google and Apple all shipped the same week, and the thesis is:
**AI stopped being a chat box and moved into physical space** — onto your face, into your
house, into your pocket. The wildcard then closes on something with no agenda at all.

### ✅ 3. Google Home opens to third-party AI agents — 2026-09-16
On **September 16, 2026** Google opened Google Home's device and data layer to **third-party

**Confidence:** High — date, price, MCP branding and the door-lock carve-out agree across

### ✅ 4. WILDCARD — the "hat" monotile twists light — 2026-09-15
**"Chiral diffraction from aperiodic monotile structure"**, published in **Nature
Communications on September 15, 2026** by researchers at the **Institute of Industrial
Science, University of Tokyo**. They fabricated structures based on the **"hat"** — the
aperiodic monotile that tiles a plane without ever repeating — and fired laser light at them.
The diffraction came back as **pinwheel-shaped patterns** with **no mirror symmetry
anywhere**, plus circular-polarization-dependent behaviour absent from conventional
quasiperiodic structures.

Backstory, verified separately: the hat was found by **David Smith, a hobbyist**, in **2022**;
he and three mathematicians published the aperiodicity proof in **March 2023**
(arXiv:2303.10798). Geometers had hunted the shape for **over 60 years**.

**Confidence:** High. Peer-reviewed, indexed in PubMed/PMC, and the backstory is
well-documented.
**Visuals:** Outstanding — the tessellation itself, laser diffraction pinwheels, macro optics.
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421593/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527405/
- https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260914102432.htm
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10798
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Smith_(amateur_mathematician)
