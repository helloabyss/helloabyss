# Packaging — Per Bank, Not Per Account

## Title

**Pick:** `FDIC Covers $250,000. Per Bank, Not Per Account.`

Named thing, hard number, and the cost it hides: people read the limit as protection per
account, and it is not.

Alternatives: `Your Broker's Cash Sweep Has A Limit You Didn't Set` · `SIPC Won't Refund A Loss. That Isn't What It's For.`

## Description

```
FDIC insurance is $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category.
Per BANK. Three accounts at the same bank in the same ownership category share one
limit, they do not each get their own.

This matters for brokerage cash sweeps. Your broker moves idle cash out to partner
banks. Spread across enough of them, each tranche sits under its own limit and the
whole balance is covered - that is the mechanism working. Concentrated at one program
bank, the excess is not covered.

The part that gets missed: your brokerage also carries SIPC, which is up to $500,000
per customer per firm with a $250,000 cash sublimit. SIPC is not FDIC. It replaces
assets that go missing if the broker fails. It does not refund a loss. If the market
falls, nothing there is covered, and that was never what it was for.

Worth doing: find the list of program banks holding your sweep. It is in your account
documents. Most people have never opened it.

Figures were checked across multiple independent sources and agree. No primary FDIC or
SIPC document was reachable at the time of writing, so none is quoted. No broker or
bank is named and no rate is given.
```

## Pinned comment

> "Ownership categories change this." Correct - the $250,000 is per depositor, per bank,
> per ownership category, so a joint account and a single account at the same bank are
> separately covered. The short says "per bank" because that is the part people get
> wrong; the category rule widens coverage, it never narrows it.
