# Late to Vice — channel plan and weekly routine

> Separate channel from the finance Shorts in this repo. The finance house style in
> `STYLE-GUIDE.md` (faceless, editorial palette, HeyGen) does **not** apply here. This is a
> streamer's channel built from real gameplay and real reactions.

## 1. The direction (one sentence)

**Late to Vice is the GTA 6 channel for everyone who's still catching up.**

The name is the angle. GTA 6 launched 19 Nov 2026 and tens of millions of players are new
to Leonida. Most big GTA channels make content for people who already know everything. You
make content for the people who got there late, and you're learning in public with them.

That one sentence is your filter. When a new idea comes up, ask:
*"Does this help or entertain someone who's behind?"* If not, it goes in the parking lot
(section 3), not the schedule.

## 2. Three content pillars (and only three)

| Pillar | What it is | Where it comes from | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Late-game lessons** | "I wasted 3 hours before I learned this." Tips, money methods, mistakes, things the game doesn't tell you | Moments on stream where you figure something out or get punished | 20–45s Shorts / TikToks |
| **2. Chaos & reactions** | Funny fails, insane police chases, glitches, chat moments | Stream highlights | 10–30s Shorts / TikToks |
| **3. Catch-up recap** | Weekly long-form: "Week 1 in Vice as a latecomer: what I learned" | Best 8–12 clips from the week, stitched with a short intro and outro | 8–15 min YouTube video |

Pillars 1 and 2 grow the channel. Pillar 3 turns Shorts viewers into subscribers and
gives you something to point to.

**Rule:** no leaked material, ever. Use your own gameplay. Take-Two hands out copyright
strikes freely, including to creators who only discussed leaks.

## 3. Taming the idea overload

You don't have too many ideas. You just have no place to put them. Use one list with
three columns and nothing else:

| PARKING LOT (any idea, no judgement) | THIS WEEK (max 5) | DONE |
|---|---|---|

- When an idea hits, it goes in **Parking lot** in under 10 seconds (Notes app is fine).
- Sunday planning: move at most **5** ideas to **This week**. Only ones that pass the
  one-sentence filter and that you have footage for (or will on the next stream).
- Everything else waits. An idea in the parking lot is not lost.

## 4. The weekly routine

Built around streaming. Total non-stream work: about **5–6 hours a week**.

| Day | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Stream days** | Stream as normal. When something good happens, **say "clip that"** out loud or drop a marker. That's your future timestamp. | — |
| **Day after each stream** | **Clip session:** scrub only the marked moments, cut 3–5 clips | 45–60 min |
| **Daily** | Post **1 Short/TikTok** (same clip to YouTube Shorts and TikTok). Reply to the first 10 comments. | 15 min |
| **Saturday** | Assemble the weekly **Catch-up recap** from the week's best clips | 2 hrs |
| **Sunday** | **Planning:** check numbers (section 7), move ≤5 ideas to "This week", pick the next stream's goal | 30 min |

Batch the clipping. Never edit on a stream day. That's where creators burn out.

**Stream with a goal.** Before each stream, pick one late-to-the-game mission, like "make
$1M with no guides" or "find every safehouse". It gives the stream a story, and clips
with a story get better retention.

## 5. Clipping for hook and retention

Every clip follows the same four beats:

1. **Hook (0–2s).** Start on the most intense frame, not the setup. Put a text overlay
   with the stakes: *"I was 3 seconds from losing $400k"*.
2. **Context (2–6s).** One line of on-screen text or voice: what you were trying to do.
3. **Payoff.** The moment itself. Cut every pause, menu and loading screen.
4. **Loop or land (last 1–2s).** End right on the reaction, or cut back into the hook
   frame so it loops. No "follow for more" outro.

**Hook text formulas that fit the brand:**
- "Nobody told me [thing] in GTA 6…"
- "Day [X] of being late to Vice: [result]"
- "Don't do what I did with [thing]"
- "I just found out you can [thing] and I'm furious"
- "Latecomer tip: [tip] (took me [time] to find)"

**Checklist before posting:**
- [ ] Something happens in the first second
- [ ] Captions burned in (most people watch muted)
- [ ] Facecam visible during the reaction, if you use one
- [ ] No dead air longer than ~1s
- [ ] Under 45s unless every second earns it
- [ ] Title / caption uses the hook text, plus "GTA 6"

## 6. Making clipping faster

- **Markers while streaming** (see section 4) are the biggest time saver. Your clip session
  becomes 5 jumps instead of a 3-hour scrub.
- **Auto-clipping tools.** OpusClip is connected to this workspace. Give it a stream VOD
  and it suggests clips with captions and reframing. Use it to make the first cut, then
  fix the hook yourself, because auto-picked starts are usually too early.
- **One caption template.** Same font, size and position every time. Set it once.
- **Download TikTok live replays right after each stream**, before they expire.

## 7. What to measure (Sunday, 10 minutes)

| Metric | Why | Rough target to aim for |
|---|---|---|
| % viewed vs. swiped away (Shorts) | Is the hook working? | Above 70% stayed to watch |
| Average % watched | Is the clip tight? | Above 80% on clips under 30s |
| Which pillar won | Do more of what works | Shift next week toward the winner |
| Subs from the weekly recap | Is long-form converting? | Track the trend, not one week |

The targets are rough starting points, not industry benchmarks. Compare yourself to your
own last four weeks.

## 8. First 7 days (start here)

1. Download every TikTok stream replay you still have.
2. Watch at 2x and note 15 timestamps where something funny, painful or useful happened.
3. Cut those into 15 clips using section 5. That's two weeks of daily posts.
4. Post one a day starting tomorrow. Same clip to TikTok and YouTube Shorts.
5. Next stream: pick a goal and use markers.
6. Saturday: first "Catch-up recap" from the best clips.
7. Sunday: first planning session.

Being late is the brand, so don't try to look like you were there from the start.
