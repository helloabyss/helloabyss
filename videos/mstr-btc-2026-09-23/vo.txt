Bitcoin is at its highest since January. Strategy's stock fell nearly eighty percent.

Strategy holds eight hundred forty five thousand bitcoin. Its average cost is about seventy five thousand four hundred dollars a coin. Bitcoin traded near eighty six thousand this week, up almost six percent, its highest since late January. So the coins are above water.

The stock is not. It is down about thirty eight percent this year. Here is the math that broke.

Strategy never traded on the coins alone. It traded on a premium to them. Analysts call it mNAV. Market value, divided by the bitcoin it holds.

In 2024 that multiple ran near three point four. Trackers now put it around one point one six. And that is the headline number. On a fully diluted basis, some trackers put it under one already. In June, the whole company was worth less than its coins.

That premium was the engine. Above one, the company could issue shares, buy more bitcoin, and raise bitcoin per share. Below one, issuing shares does the opposite. The flywheel ran on the premium, not on the coin.

So the company started selling. A July filing reported three thousand five hundred eighty eight coins sold, for about two hundred sixteen million dollars. Across 2026, roughly six thousand nine hundred coins. Most of it below its own average cost.

Here is the part people miss. Bitcoin rallying does not repair this. The coin went up. The premium did not come back.

So if you follow this company, the number to watch is not the bitcoin price. It is bitcoin per share, and the multiple. One tells you what they own. The other tells you what you are paying for it.
