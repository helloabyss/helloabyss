# Procreate → animated video

How hand-drawn iPad art gets into a video here, and how it moves once it is in.
Written 2026-10-05. **Proven end to end** — `engine/rig-demo.webm` was rendered
by this pipeline.

---

## The short answer

Procreate exports transparent PNGs. The engine composites PNGs and animates
them per frame with CSS transforms, then pipes the frames to ffmpeg. So a
Procreate drawing is a drop-in replacement for any part the engine draws —
**provided it lands on the right canvas with the right pivot.**

Registration is the whole problem. Hand-drawn art only animates cleanly if
every redraw sits in the same place. That is what `engine/parts/manifest.json`
is for: it is a contract, not a cache.

## Two ways art comes in

**1. Replace a rig part** — Zul's head, torso, arm, leg. These animate.
**2. Drop in a prop or hero plate** — a chart, a receipt, a building. These sit
in a scene and get panned or scaled, but have no joints.

Route 2 needs nothing but a transparent PNG. Route 1 needs the contract below.

## The contract — canvas sizes and pivots

Export templates are in `engine/parts/`. Each PNG is rendered at 2× so it stays
crisp when the rig scales it up.

| Part | **Procreate canvas** | Pivot, in that canvas | What the pivot is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `head` | **600 × 600** | 300, 392 | base of the skull |
| `torso` | **760 × 400** | 380, 28 | neck joint |
| `arm` | **220 × 420** | 110, 44 | shoulder end |
| `leg` | **220 × 500** | 110, 36 | hip |

Pivots above are in 2× pixels, matching the canvas. `manifest.json` stores the
1× numbers plus `pivot_local`, which is the pivot in **Zul's own coordinate
space** — that is what the rig assembles against, because canvas pixels differ
per part and local coordinates do not. Pinning parts by guessed canvas offsets
is what produced a floating head on the first attempt.

### Procreate settings

- **New canvas at the size above.** Not "a bit bigger and crop later" — the
  crop is the registration.
- Delete the background layer, or hide it, so the export has real alpha.
- Draw the existing part PNG in as a reference layer at 100% opacity, trace
  over it, then delete that layer. That guarantees the pivot still works.
- **Share → PNG.** Not JPEG (no alpha), not Share Layers.
- Drop the file into `engine/parts/`, overwriting. Nothing else changes.

Re-run `python3 rig.py` and the rig picks it up. There is no import step.

## Animating it — three options

| | How | Effort | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Transform the whole drawing** | Engine pans/zooms/rotates a static PNG per frame | Lowest | Props, hero plates, establishing shots |
| **B. Cut-out rig** ⭐ | Parts on pivots, engine rotates each per frame | Low once drawn | **Zul talking, pointing, gesturing — the default** |
| **C. Frame-by-frame** | Procreate *Animation Assist*, export PNG sequence, engine plays it | Highest | A short hero moment. Never a whole video |

**B is the one to build on.** You draw each part once; the engine produces
unlimited motion from them for free. `engine/rig.py` is a working example — six
parts, three poses, eased between, with a head bob driving a counter-rotation
in the legs.

A pose is three numbers per limb:

```js
{ armL:[ 4,  6], armR:[  4, 52], head:[0,-3], bob:1.0 }   // raise to point
```

Render with `node render_rig.js <seconds> <fps>`.

### Procreate's own animation, honestly

**Animation Assist** exports a PNG sequence and that is genuinely useful for a
2-second hero beat — a coin flipping, a line being drawn. It is the wrong tool
for 90 seconds of hosting: you would be drawing ~2,000 frames.

**Procreate Dreams** exports finished video and bypasses this pipeline
entirely. If a sequence is easier to animate there, animate it there and treat
the export as an asset to cut in. Do not try to rebuild Dreams here.

**The time-lapse replay** every Procreate canvas records is the one worth
planning around. A series whose promise is *showing the working* has an obvious
use for footage of the working being drawn by hand. That is a genuine format
asset, not a gimmick — see `SERIES-ONE-NUMBER.md` §1.

## The rule that protects the character

**Draw once, reuse forever.**

The reason Zul is generated by `engine/zul.py` is that he is then identical in
every episode. Hand-drawing him per episode throws that away — a character that
drifts is not a character. So:

- Redraw a part **once**, commit the PNG, and reuse it. Do not redraw per video.
- Keep the generated version as the fallback; `parts.py` regenerates it.
- Episode-specific art belongs in **props**, never in the host.

## Palette

Procreate will happily paint outside the channel's three colours. The locked
set, from `STYLE-GUIDE.md` plus Zul's beanie:

```
#000000  line / near-black        #d81e28  the one red accent
#ffffff  paper                    #f2c200  beanie yellow  (Zul only)
#b9c7d0  lens tint                #2e9e3e  beanie green   (Zul only)
```

Build these as a Procreate palette once and select from it only. Everything
outside this set is off-style, and `vidiq_score_thumbnail` will push you
further out — `CLAUDE.md` already says not to chase that score.

## What is proven, and what is not

**Proven:** transparent PNG export, pivot registration, cut-out animation,
frame-accurate render to WebM. `engine/rig-demo.webm` — 180 frames, 24 fps,
1080×1920, rendered from four part PNGs.

**Not proven:**
- No Procreate file has been through this. The parts were generated from
  `zul.py` to stand in. The contract is what matters and it is exercised, but
  the first real drawing may reveal alpha or trim surprises.
- **No audio.** The bundled ffmpeg has two muxers and no audio codec. Sound is
  muxed server-side via an ElevenLabs composition node — free, and documented
  in `engine/VIDEO.md`.
- WebM out only. The composition step converts to H.264 MP4 as a side effect.
- The rig has no forearm or elbow. Arms are single segments that rotate at the
  shoulder. An elbow means splitting `arm` into two parts and a second pivot.
