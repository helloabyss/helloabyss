## VO — verbatim

> SpaceX burned fourteen billion dollars of cash last year. The market says it is worth two trillion.

Both of those numbers come from the same filing. Only one of them made it into
the videos.

Here is another. Micron's gross margin doubled in twelve months — forty percent
to eighty-one. And Bloom Energy raised its own revenue forecast three times in
nine months, which is the corporate equivalent of repeatedly underestimating
how hungry you are.

All three companies are real. All three are genuinely wired into the biggest
capital build-out in a generation, and I am not here to talk you out of any of
them.

I am here because each one has a number its fans do not mention. All three of
those numbers are sitting in public filings. And all three took about ten
minutes to find.

So: the bull case, from the documents. Then the line underneath it.

### One. **SPCX**

SpaceX listed on the Nasdaq on the twelfth of June, twenty twenty-six, under
the ticker **S-P-C-X**. It priced at a hundred and thirty-five dollars, opened
at a hundred and fifty, and closed its first day at a hundred and sixty-one.
That put it at roughly two point one trillion dollars on day one.

Most people still file SpaceX under "rocket company". The filing does not
agree. In twenty twenty-five the company booked eighteen point seven billion
dollars of revenue, and eleven point four billion of that — about sixty-one
percent — came from connectivity. That is Starlink. SpaceX is a broadband
company with a very good launch division attached.

And the launch division is good. Before twenty-fifteen the standard way to end
a rocket launch was to throw the rocket away. Imagine buying a jet, flying it
to Paris, and leaving it there. Reusability is the reason the economics changed
at all.

But Starlink is the engine. Subscribers went two point three million in
twenty twenty-three, four point four million in twenty-four, eight point nine
million in twenty-five. Ten point three million by March. Around twelve million
by June. Starlink made four point four billion dollars of operating income last
year on a thirty-nine percent margin.

> **The number the bull case leaves out.**

Two of them, actually.

The first is in the company's own quarterly discussion. In the first quarter of
twenty twenty-six, subscribers grew a hundred and five percent. Revenue per
subscriber fell almost twenty-three percent. Monthly revenue per user has gone
from about ninety-nine dollars in twenty twenty-three to about sixty-six
dollars. Growth is real, and some of it is being bought with price cuts.

The second is larger. In twenty twenty-five SpaceX generated six point eight
billion dollars of cash from operations and spent twenty point seven billion on
capital expenditure. That is negative fourteen billion dollars of free cash
flow in a single year.

Satellites are not a one-off purchase. They de-orbit, and they have to be
replaced. A constellation is a subscription the company pays, not just one it
collects. None of that makes **SPCX** a bad business. It makes it a business
that is still buying its own future, in cash, right now.

### Two. **BE**

Bloom Energy joined the S&P 500 on the twenty-first of September.

What it sells is unglamorous and currently in violent demand: on-site fuel
cells. A data centre needs power, the grid queue is long, and Bloom sells a box
that generates electricity next to the building instead of waiting for a
utility.

The growth is not subtle. First quarter revenue was about seven hundred and
fifty-one million dollars, up a hundred and thirty percent. Second quarter was
about one point zero seven billion, up a hundred and sixty-six percent. The
company raised full-year guidance three times — three point one to three point
three billion in February, three point four to three point eight in April,
three point nine to four point two in July. Raising guidance twice in a year is
unusual. Three times is a company being overtaken by its own order book.

> **The number the bull case leaves out.**

That order book is the thing to look at properly.

The backlog is about twenty billion dollars. You will hear that number a lot.
About six billion of it is product orders; the other fourteen billion is
long-term service. Those service contracts run five to twenty years — and
customers can cancel annually.

A backlog is not a bank balance. It is a list of people who currently intend to
buy something. Twenty billion dollars of intent, cancellable once a year, is a
genuinely strong position and it is not the same thing as twenty billion
dollars. Anyone quoting the backlog without the cancellation clause is quoting
half a sentence.

### Three. **MU**

Back to the number we opened with.

Micron's financial year twenty twenty-six ended on the third of September.
Revenue was a hundred and thirty-three point two billion dollars. The year
before, it was thirty-seven point four billion. That is growth of two hundred
and fifty-six percent — in one year, at a company more than forty years old.

Net income was eighty-five billion dollars. Earnings per share, seventy-four
dollars and thirty-three cents. Gross margin went from thirty-nine point eight
percent to eighty point seven percent.

The reason is high-bandwidth memory. An AI accelerator is useless without
memory sitting next to it, very few companies can make the high-end kind, and
demand has outrun supply.

> **The number the bull case leaves out.**

Here I want to correct something, because it is circulating and it is wrong.

Eighty-one percent is Micron's **gross** margin. It is not what the company
keeps. After everything, net margin is about sixty-four percent — I calculated
that from net income over revenue, it is not a line in the release. Sixty-four
percent is a remarkable number. It is not eighty-one, and if you are modelling
on eighty-one you are modelling a company that does not exist.

And here is the comparison that should make you careful rather than excited.

Nvidia — the company every one of these chips plugs into, and the one the
market is pricing to be the first ever worth six trillion dollars — ran a
seventy-five percent gross margin last quarter. Micron ran eighty-one across
the whole year. That is a year against a quarter, and it still holds: Nvidia's
own full year was seventy-one.

So a memory manufacturer is currently earning more per dollar of sales than
the company whose accelerators it exists to feed. That is genuinely true today.
Ask what has to stay true for it to last. Memory has to remain scarcer than
the chips that need it — in an industry where every producer on earth is
currently building capacity as fast as it can pour concrete.

The second thing is the shape of the business. Memory is the most reliably
cyclical industry in technology. It has made people rich roughly every seven
years and then quietly un-made them, because the cure for high prices is high
prices: every producer expands at the top, supply arrives late, and margins go
back down. Eighty percent gross margins are not a new normal. They are what the
top of a cycle looks like.

And note what the next number actually is. Micron guided to sixty-one and a
half billion dollars for the coming quarter. Guidance is a forecast. It is the
company's estimate, not money it has made, and it should never be quoted as
though it already happened.

### The pattern

Three companies, three real theses, three numbers the enthusiasm skips: a
cash-flow deficit, a cancellation clause, and a cycle.

None of those make these bad businesses. **SPCX**, **BE** and **MU** are all
genuinely plugged into the largest capital build-out in a generation. The point
is narrower and more useful than that: the bull case and the risk come from the
same document, and only one of them tends to make it into the video you watched
about it.

Here is the thing you can actually do. All three figures I used as the
counterweight — the free cash flow line, the cancellation terms, the difference
between gross and net margin — are in filings that take about ten minutes to
find and are free to read. Before you buy anything off the back of a video,
including this one, open the last quarterly release and find the number nobody
quoted.

If it is not there, that is your answer.
