# STYLE — "This Week in the Market" series bible

Weekly faceless US-market recap. **No presenter, no avatar, no face, ever.** Every second is
voiceover over charts, data visualization, B-roll and kinetic text.

**This file inherits `STYLE-GUIDE.md`** — the channel's locked visual identity (imagery-first
three-layer stack, three-colour palette, dramatic motion, hard constraints). That file is
channel-wide and still binding. This one adds what a *recurring market show* needs on top:
episode architecture, scope discipline, and a chart grammar.

Companion files: `VO_PROFILE.md` (how it sounds — locked), `COMPLIANCE.md` (what may be
said — non-negotiable).

---

## 1. Scope discipline — the rule that defines the series

> **The subject is always US stocks.**

Fed decisions, macro prints, geopolitics and China do not get covered because they are
important. They earn their place **by moving US equities**.

**The one-sentence test.** Every beat must connect to US equities in a single sentence
naming two things: **a specific US ticker, sector or index**, and **the transmission
mechanism**.

- ✅ "The PBOC's cut lifted copper, and that pushed Freeport up four percent."
- ✅ "New export licence rules hit Nvidia's China data-center revenue line."
- ❌ "Tensions rose in the South China Sea this week." *(no ticker, no mechanism — cut)*
- ❌ "China's economy is slowing." *(no ticker, no mechanism — cut)*

**If a story cannot pass that test in one sentence, it does not go in the episode.** Not
shortened, not hedged into vagueness — cut. An interesting story with no equity
transmission belongs in a different video.

Every beat in the shot list carries its ticker/sector/index in the beat header, so the test
is visible at a glance and cannot be skipped.

### The China lens

China is the recurring international lens — but it is subject to the same test, and to
`COMPLIANCE.md §5` (describe, never editorialize; flag disputed data). The recurring
channels through which China reaches US equities:

| Channel | Typical US read-through |
|---|---|
| PBOC and policy decisions | rates, commodities, industrials, miners |
| Export controls and tariffs, **both directions** | semis, capital goods, retail import costs |
| Rare earths and supply chain | autos, defence, hardware |
| Chinese demand data | US multinationals' revenue lines — luxury, autos, machinery, staples |
| Semis and chip restrictions | Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Applied Materials, Lam, KLA |
| Chinese listings and delisting risk | ADRs, index inclusion, the brokers that carry them |
| Yuan moves | repricing US multinationals' translated earnings |

Name the channel out loud in the narration. "Through the supply chain" is the sentence that
earns the beat its place.

## 2. Episode architecture

**Runtime 110–130s · 275–325 words** (`VO_PROFILE.md §4`). Never pad to fill, never cut a
fact to fit. Under 170s hard ceiling.

| # | Beat | Time | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Compliance card** | 0:00–0:04 | — | "Educational only. Not financial advice." over the cold open. Not a separate shot. |
| 1 | **Cold open — the week in one number** | 0:00–0:08 | ~20 | The single number that defined the week, on screen at full frame. No greeting, no "welcome back", no channel name. |
| 2 | **The tape** | 0:08–0:20 | ~30 | Index levels and the week's move. Where the money actually went — sector leadership and breadth. |
| 3 | **Fed and rates** | 0:20–0:42 | ~55 | What changed vs. what was expected. Curve move, what futures now price. |
| 4 | **Macro data** | 0:42–1:02 | ~50 | Print vs. consensus, the surprise, the equity reaction. |
| 5 | **Earnings** | 1:02–1:28 | ~65 | The reports that moved index weights or repriced a sector. |
| 6 | **Geopolitics / China** | 1:28–1:48 | ~50 | Event → mechanism → named US ticker. |
| 7 | **The week ahead** | 1:48–2:00 | ~30 | What prints and who reports. Calendar, not prediction. |
| 8 | **Close + disclaimer card** | 2:00–2:05 | ~10 | One line the viewer can use. Full disclaimer card holds to the end. |

**Segments flex.** A quiet Fed week and a loud earnings week redistribute the word budget —
that is normal. What does **not** flex: the compliance card, the close card, and the scope
test on every beat. A segment with nothing that passes the test is **dropped entirely**
rather than filled with filler.

**No cold-open hype.** The hook is a number, a level or a reversal — never "markets went
CRAZY this week". The tone is `STYLE-GUIDE.md`'s dry analytical newsroom.

### The mandatory counter-beat
`STYLE-GUIDE.md` requires counter-evidence in every short, and it applies here. At least one
beat per episode carries the disconfirming read: the print that contradicts the narrative,
the beat that was bought and sold off anyway, the guidance that contradicts the quarter.
**An episode where every data point agrees is a red flag that something was left out.**

## 3. Visual system

`STYLE-GUIDE.md` governs the look. What follows is how a **data show** lives inside it.

### The stack, applied to market content

| Layer | In this series |
|---|---|
| **BASE** | Cinematic photographic B-roll, graded dark and desaturated. Still the foundation — a chart is never alone on a flat background. |
| **MID** | The chart / data visualization, composited over the plate with real depth and parallax. |
| **TOP** | Hero number, labels, and burned-in word-by-word captions. |

> **The failure mode this series must avoid: a slideshow of charts.** A chart floating on
> near-black is the "typography on abstract backgrounds" mistake in a new costume — the
> exact thing `STYLE-GUIDE.md` was written to stop. Every chart sits **over a plate**: the
> trading-floor ceiling, the server room, the port crane, the Fed facade. The chart is
> glass in front of a scene, not a PowerPoint slide.

### Chart grammar

- **One idea per chart.** Two series maximum on a line chart; if a third is needed, it is a
  second chart.
- **Charts draw themselves** left to right, 0.8–1.5s. Never fade in complete.
- **Numbers count up** to their value (`STYLE-GUIDE.md`). Never cut to a final figure.
- **Every chart carries its as-of stamp and source**, small, bottom-left, always legible.
  This is `COMPLIANCE.md §7` rendered — not decoration.
- **Y-axis starts at a labelled value, never at an unlabelled crop.** A truncated axis that
  exaggerates a move is a compliance problem, not a design choice.
- **Structural failure on negative beats** (`STYLE-GUIDE.md`): the line cracks and drops,
  debris falls. Reserved for genuinely negative data — not applied to every down-tick.
- Chart types: **line** for levels/time, **bar** for surprise vs. consensus, **curve plot**
  for the yield curve, **grid of tiles** for sector breadth, **single hero number** for the
  cold open. Nothing else without a reason.

### Colour — and why there is no green

The palette is locked at **near-black, white, one red accent**. A market show wants
red/green for up/down. **It does not get it.**

- **Red = the accent.** It marks the one thing the beat is about — a level, a move, an
  annotation. It is *emphasis*, not *direction*.
- **Up moves are white.** Down moves are red **only when the fall is the point of the beat**.
- **Direction is carried by shape, not colour** — the sign (`+`/`−`), the arrow, the slope of
  the line, the position of the bar against its zero rule.
- Never more than **one red element on screen at a time**. Red everywhere is red nowhere.

> *Known tension:* viewers are conditioned to expect green for up. The mitigation is that
> sign and slope do the work, which they do in print finance. This is a deliberate choice in
> favour of the channel's identity — the same reasoning as `CLAUDE.md`'s instruction not to
> chase vidIQ's saturation-rewarding thumbnail score.

**Working palette values** (pin them here so every episode matches; replace if exact brand
hexes exist):

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--ink` | `#0B0B0C` | Background, grade floor |
| `--paper` | `#F5F5F3` | Type, primary series, up moves |
| `--accent` | `#D42A2A` | The one accent. Emphasis and negative beats |
| `--muted` | `#6E6E6E` | Secondary series, axes, gridlines, source stamps |

Gridlines and axes sit at low contrast — the data is the brightest thing in the frame.

### B-roll vocabulary

Recurring plates, reused across episodes so the series reads as one thing. **No faces, no
identifiable people, no real corporate logos** (`STYLE-GUIDE.md`).

| Segment | Plates |
|---|---|
| Fed / rates | Institutional stone facade, low angle · empty marble corridor · document under lamplight · microphone on an empty lectern (no speaker) |
| Macro data | Port cranes at dusk · warehouse aisles · empty retail floor after hours · housing frames against sky · freight yard |
| Earnings | Server rooms · fab cleanroom corridors · automated production lines · shipping containers |
| China | Container terminal, long lens · rare-earth ore macro · cargo rail · currency paper macro · harbour at night |
| The tape | Trading-floor ceiling geometry · fibre and cabling macro · glass tower reflections at dusk |

**Bookend the episode** (`STYLE-GUIDE.md`): the same plate opens and closes, ideally from an
identical camera position. Default series bookend is the trading-floor ceiling, so the show
has a spine week to week.

**Hero shot:** one tactile macro per episode, tied to the week's biggest story — the ore, the
connector, the paper, the crane locking.

### Motion
Per `STYLE-GUIDE.md`: cut or punch in every 1.2–1.8s, no shot past 2s, continuous motion,
parallax, no plain cuts. Applies to charts too — the camera moves **through** the chart
plane, past the axis, between the bars. A static chart held for four seconds is off-style.

## 4. Writing rules

- **Second person sparingly, and never as instruction.** "Your portfolio should" is banned
  by `COMPLIANCE.md`.
- **The channel has no opinion.** It has the tape, the print, and named analysts.
- **Numbers spelled out in the VO, precise on screen** (`VO_PROFILE.md §4`).
- **8–10 words per sentence**, one idea each, number at the end of the sentence.
- **Two em-dashes per script, maximum** — they measurably cost runtime (`VO_PROFILE.md §4`).
- **No greeting, no sign-off branding, no subscribe ask** (`STYLE-GUIDE.md`: no follow-bait).
- **Close on something the viewer can use** — what to watch, when it prints, what would
  change the read. Never "let me know in the comments".

### Recurring phrasings — the series' spine
Reuse these so the show reads as one voice week to week:
- "Against a consensus of …" (the surprise framing)
- "The tape's read was …" (market reaction, not the channel's view)
- "Futures now price …" (never "the market expects")
- "The line from the call was …" (the one quote that explains the move)
- "Through the supply chain / through the revenue line" (the China mechanism)
- "That's the read as of Friday's close." (the as-of, spoken)

## 5. Packaging

- **Title:** the week's single biggest US-equity fact. No "SHOCKING", no "what they don't
  want you to know" (`CLAUDE.md`). Date-anchored so the series is navigable.
- **Description:** two-sentence summary → **source list with as-of dates** → verbatim
  disclaimer from `COMPLIANCE.md §8`.
- **Pinned comment:** the caveat most likely to become the top comment — the disputed figure,
  the estimate that isn't a price, the number that has since moved.
- **Thumbnail:** honours the palette. Do not chase the vidIQ saturation score (`CLAUDE.md`).
- **Hashtags:** `#stockmarket #investing #fed #earnings #stocks`

## 6. Definition of done

An episode ships only when:
1. Every beat passes the one-sentence scope test (§1), visible in the beat header.
2. `COMPLIANCE.md §9` checklist is complete with **no `[OPEN]` flags**.
3. Every number traces to a primary document in `research/YYYY-MM-DD.md` with an as-of.
4. At least one counter-beat is present (§2).
5. Word count is inside the `VO_PROFILE.md §4` budget for the target runtime.
6. Compliance card at 0:00–0:04; full disclaimer card on the final frame.
7. `ARCHIVE.md` updated so next week does not repeat this week's framing.
8. The render is reported **complete, not verified**, with a verification checklist
   (`VO_PROFILE.md §5`).
