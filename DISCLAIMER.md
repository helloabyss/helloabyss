## Where each version goes

| Placement | Text | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Video description** | The full text above, verbatim | Every video, every time. Bottom of the description |
| **On-screen card, first 4 seconds** | `Educational only. Not financial advice.` | The full paragraph is unreadable at speed. The short card is the on-screen form; it does not replace the description text |
| **Pinned comment** | Not required | Keep the pinned comment for the strongest objection to the video's weakest claim |

The short on-screen card and the long description disclaimer are **both** required. One is not a substitute for the other.

## Topic-specific lines to add above it

These sit **above** the disclaimer, not inside it — never edit the canonical text to add them.

- **Derivatives / options:** Options can expire worthless and lose 100% of the premium paid. Some strategies carry unlimited loss. Not suitable for all investors.
- **Crypto:** Digital assets are highly volatile and total loss of capital is possible. Assets held on an exchange may not be recoverable if that exchange fails.
- **A named individual or company:** All statements about named people and companies are drawn from the public record, with sources linked above.

## Scope note

This is the user-supplied disclaimer, stored as given. I am not a lawyer and have not assessed whether it is enforceable in any particular jurisdiction, nor whether a release-of-claims clause binds a viewer who merely watches a video. If the channel is monetised at scale, takes sponsorships, or ever discusses specific securities, it is worth having a solicitor review it once.

A disclaimer limits exposure; it does not cure an inaccurate claim. The accuracy rules in `prompts/finance-production-system.md` are the actual protection.
