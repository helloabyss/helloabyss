Inflation, explained with honey.
One hive. One hundred jars of honey. One hundred wax tokens.
One token buys one jar.
Now the hive council makes a hundred more tokens.
Same honey. Twice the tokens.
Every bee feels richer, so every bee wants more honey.
But there are still only a hundred jars. So the price goes up. Two tokens a jar.
That's demand-pull inflation. Too many tokens chasing the same honey.
Now flip it. A bad summer. Fewer flowers. Only sixty jars.
Same tokens, less honey. The price climbs again. That's cost-push.
Here's the part that stings. Tokens sitting still buy a little less honey every year.
In the human hive, prices rose three point four percent in the twelve months to August.
So the question isn't just whether you save. It's whether your savings grow faster than the price of honey.
