The ten-year Treasury yield closed above five percent on Friday. That number sits underneath the price of every American stock.

The week split three ways. The Dow fell seven hundred and thirty-three points, its worst week since March. The Nasdaq rose two point six percent. The S&P 500 added zero point seven.

Friday's closes. The S&P 500 at seven thousand six hundred fifty. The Nasdaq at twenty-six thousand five hundred twenty-three. The Dow at fifty-one thousand six hundred eighty-three.

On Wednesday the Federal Reserve raised interest rates. A quarter point, to a range of three point seven five to four percent. It is the first increase since 2023. The vote was twelve to nothing. Not one dissent.

The chair is Kevin Warsh now. He took over from Jerome Powell in May. Warsh said the committee wants a timelier return to two percent inflation.

Here is the part that complicates it. Inflation did not accelerate into this decision. August consumer prices ran at three point four percent. That was unchanged from July, and in line with what forecasters expected. The Fed tightened anyway.

The market did not read it as a simple squeeze either. Wednesday sold off. Thursday bought it back. The Nasdaq finished the week higher while the Dow had its worst week since March. Higher rates pressed on the two ends of the index differently.

Next week, Micron reports fiscal fourth quarter results on September thirtieth. That is the clearest read available on memory pricing and AI capital spending.

One number to watch. If the ten-year holds above five percent, then the hurdle every US stock is priced against has moved. That is the read as of Friday's close.
