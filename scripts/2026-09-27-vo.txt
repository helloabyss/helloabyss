Every major index closed the week higher. But the Nasdaq rose two point one percent, and the Dow just zero point three. Seven to one.

And it happened while the ten-year Treasury yield hit five point one eight percent, its highest since 2007.

A higher risk-free rate should pull stock valuations down. This week, it didn't.

What moved was the Fed. Hot activity data and rising inflation expectations lifted the odds of an October rate hike to fifty-eight percent, from forty-two a week earlier. That is market pricing, not a forecast.

Next week, five numbers decide it. Job openings on Tuesday. Core PCE inflation and Micron's earnings on Wednesday. The ISM factory survey on Thursday. And payrolls on Friday.

If inflation runs hot, the hike case hardens. If Micron guides soft, the AI trade loses its anchor. If payrolls come in weak, growth is what cracks.
