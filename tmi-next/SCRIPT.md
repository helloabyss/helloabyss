### Conflicts carried into the script, and how they were handled

- **C1 — $145,000 vs $150,000.** Multiple credible law-firm alerts (Lathrop GPM, Baker
  Donelson, Groom, Trucker Huss) still print **$145,000**. They predate the 2025-11-13 IRS
  release and reflect the un-indexed statutory base. **Script uses $150,000.** This is the
  single most likely way this video gets a number wrong.
- **C2 — W-2 Box 3 vs Box 5.** Sources genuinely conflict. The distinction does not bite at a
  $150,000 test, because the 2025 Box 3 cap is $176,100. **The script says "Social Security
  wages" and never names a box number** — on frame or in the VO.
- **C3 — "effective 2026" vs "regs apply 2027."** The statutory mandate bites 2026-01-01; the
  final regulations' own applicability date is tax years beginning after 2026-12-31, with
  good-faith compliance for 2026. **The script says "your twenty twenty-six catch-up" and never
  says "the IRS rules take effect in 2026."**
- **Edge case at exactly $150,000.** Single-sourced and unresolved. **The script says "more
  than that," which sidesteps it.**

### Deliberately excluded

Any security, fund, ticker, plan provider or price · any buy/sell/hold framing · any dollar
figure for "tax lost" (that is arithmetic off a marginal-rate assumption, not a fact) ·
"$145,000" · the Box 3 / Box 5 question · the Federal Register date · the two-jobs example
(the controlled-group aggregation carve-out makes it unsafe at this length) · the SIMPLE/SEP
carve-out (single-sourced) · the 2023 "55% of over-$150k earners" figure (stale, single-sourced)
· any claim that Social Security wages are "always higher than you think" — Section 125
cafeteria deductions push the other way.

## Counter-evidence — stated out loud, not buried

Two, both in the VO:

1. **"Roth is a timing shift, not a tax."** The deduction is gone this year, but qualified
   withdrawals and all their earnings come out tax-free. For anyone expecting a similar or
   higher rate in retirement, forced Roth is a gain. The honest frame is surprise and cash
   flow, never loss.
2. **"Only about seventeen percent of eligible savers make one."** This touches a slice of a
   slice: catch-up-eligible, actually contributing a catch-up, **and** over the wage line at a
   single employer. It also does not touch anyone under fifty, 2026 new hires at a new
   employer, the self-employed with no W-2 wages, or anyone contributing under the standard
   deferral limit.

The script's sharpest claim — the lockout — is the one place the harm is real and larger than
the deduction, and it is stated conditionally: *a plan with no Roth option.*

## Titles — proven shape: named thing + one hard number + the cost the number hides

1. **`$150,000 — And It Isn't Your Salary. The 2026 Catch-Up Rule.`**
   Best. The surprise is the mechanism error, which is what makes it worth thirty seconds.
2. **`Your 401(k) Catch-Up Goes Roth-Only Above $150,000 In 2026`**
   Safest. Named thing, hard number, no loss implication.
3. **`$8,000 Of Catch-Up — Gone, If Your Plan Has No Roth Option`**
   Sharpest. It is the lockout, which is the genuinely larger harm, and the conditional is in
   the title so it does not overclaim.

Avoid any title using the words "lost its deduction" without the conditional — it reads as a
loss claim, and this is a timing shift.

> "So I lose the tax break." Not quite — it is a timing shift. The deduction goes, but
