## VO SCRIPT (170 words)

**[0:00–0:05] HOOK**
> The most quoted story in tech investing never happened.

**[0:05–0:18] THE STORY AS TOLD**
> You've heard it. Eighteen ninety-four. The Times of London predicts every street will be
> buried under nine feet of manure within fifty years. The world's urban planners meet,
> find no answer, and give up. Then the automobile arrives and saves everyone.

**[0:18–0:32] THE TURN**
> No one has ever found that article. The Times searched its own archive and denied it.
> The whole thing — the quote, the fifty years, the conference collapsing in despair —
> traces back to a single essay published in 2004.

**[0:32–0:48] BUT THE REAL PART**
> But the horses were real. London kept around three hundred thousand of them, dropping
> close to a thousand tons of dung a day. New York still had a hundred and twenty-eight
> thousand horses in 1900.

**[0:48–0:58] AND IT DID END**
> And it did end fast. By 1912, cars outnumbered horses in New York. The last horse-drawn
> streetcar ran in 1917.

**[0:58–1:05] CLOSE**
> The problem was real. The prophecy was invented. When someone quotes a parable to end an
> argument, ask where it came from.

## FACT BASE

| Claim | Detail | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Nine feet / 50 years / Times of London 1894" quote | No such article located in the archive | **High** — widely documented as unsourced |
| Origin of the quote | Stephen Davies, "The Great Horse-Manure Crisis of 1894", FEE, September 2004 | **High** |
| The Times' response | Refuted the attribution in 2018 | **High** |
| What the real June 8, 1894 Times piece said | Complained about dust and mud, not manure; predicted no crisis | **Medium-high** |
| 1898 first international urban planning conference, NYC | The conference was real and manure was a dominant topic | **High** |
| "Conference abandoned after 3 days in despair" | **Unverified / part of the invented framing.** Script says "the conference collapsing in despair" is part of what traces to 2004 — it does not assert the conference never happened | **Deliberately hedged** |
| London horses | ~300,000; ~1,000 tons of dung per day by the 1890s | **Medium** — commonly cited, secondary sources |
| NYC horses 1900 | ~128,000 | **Medium** |
| NYC + Brooklyn 1880 | 150,000–175,000 horses; 3–4 million lbs manure/day | **Medium** (not used in VO) |
| Cars outnumber horses in NYC | By 1912 | **Medium** |
| Last horse-drawn streetcar | Bleecker Street Line, ceased 26 July 1917 | **High** |

### Editorial note
The script deliberately does **not** claim the 1898 conference never happened — it did, and
manure dominated it. What traces to 2004 is the *dramatised* version: the Times quote, the
fifty-year figure, and the conference giving up in despair. The VO wording ("the quote, the
fifty years, the conference collapsing in despair") is precise about this. Do not loosen it
in any re-cut — the looser claim would be false.
