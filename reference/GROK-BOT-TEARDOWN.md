## What it actually does

1. **Real B-roll is the base layer** — macro of circuit boards, a hand soldering, a drive
   being opened, a workbench. Continuous motion inside the shot, which is why a 3.3s hold
   never feels static. The motion is in the *footage*, not in the camera move.
2. **Zul is a small persistent corner host** — bottom-left/right, roughly 15% of frame
   height, re-posed shot to shot (pointing, gesturing, hands clasped). He is an overlay on
   footage, never the subject, never on a flat ground. This is the reconciliation of "Zul"
   with "imagery-first" that we got wrong: he is furniture, not the scene.
3. **Data is charted, not announced.** Dark rounded panels with a thin amber border hold
   real candlesticks with dated axes (`Oct 12 … Oct 19`), series labelled `DRAM · daily`,
   `MU · 10 days`, `SMH · 10 days`. Two panels stack when two series are compared.
4. **Sourcing is on-screen.** `Oct 19 close · Webull` under each chart;
   `Photo: CXMT via Tom's Hardware, Oct 13` under an embedded photo card.
5. **Two-sided evidence as a card pair** — `Rosenblatt "overblown"` against
   `Goldman concern warranted`. Our counter-evidence rule, done as a visual.
6. **Headlines are a serif display face** in amber/gold, with red reserved for the negative
   ("2 red days", "not shipped"). Captions are small monospace in a dark pill, bottom-centre.
7. **The host names himself** — "I'm Zul." on an otherwise empty macro shot.
